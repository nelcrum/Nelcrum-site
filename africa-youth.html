Insights|Africa's youth

The continent the chart left out

A widely shared chart on youth unemployment and Gen Z protest compared France, China, the U.K., the U.S. and Germany. Africa, the youngest continent and home to several of the protests, was missing. Put it back and the story changes.

By Dontá Council, PhDNovember 2026Data refreshed monthly, last on November 19, 2026

Of the five economies in the chart, France had the highest youth unemployment rate, at 18.9%. Among the 51 African countries with an estimate, 16 have a higher rate than France. South Africa's is 59.9%.

The whole continent

Ranking every African country with an estimate against the five in the original chart shows how much the comparison was missing. The highest rates cluster in Southern and North Africa. Many of the lowest are in West Africa, for reasons the next chart explains.

Low unemployment, high precarity

Several countries where Gen Z protests toppled or shook governments report youth unemployment below Germany's. That is not a sign of health. The unemployment rate counts only people with no work at all who are looking for it. Where there is no unemployment insurance, few young people can afford to be idle, so they take whatever informal work exists.

Madagascar makes the point. Youth-led protests there ended Andry Rajoelina's presidency in October 2025, yet youth unemployment is just 5.2%. The telling number is that 84.4% of all workers are in vulnerable employment: working for themselves or unpaid in a family business, usually without a contract or social protection.

The youngest region on earth

In Sub-Saharan Africa, 40.4% of the population is under 15. In the European Union the figure is 14.2%. These are the people who will enter the labor market over the next decade, which is why youth labor conditions on the continent matter well beyond it.

Methods and data

Data. All labor and population figures come from the World Bank's World Development Indicators, retrieved through its public API. Youth unemployment and vulnerable employment are International Labour Organization modeled estimates, which fill gaps between national labor-force surveys. For countries with few recent surveys, such as Djibouti, Libya and Somalia, treat single-year values as approximate. Africa here means the World Bank's Sub-Saharan Africa region plus the six African members of its Middle East and North Africa region: Algeria, Djibouti, Egypt, Libya, Morocco and Tunisia.

Updates. The data are checked monthly. When the World Bank revises its estimates, the changes are reviewed before publication, and the figures in this text update with them. World Bank figures last updated November 11, 2026.

What is missing. The original chart's companion graphic used Eurostat data on young adults living with their parents. No comparable, annually updated series exists across African countries, so it is not reproduced here.
