The continent the chart left out

A widely shared chart on youth unemployment and Gen Z protest compared France, China, the U.K., the U.S. and Germany. Africa, the youngest continent and home to several of the protests, was not on it. Here is what the same data shows when it is.

By Dontá Council, PhD · November 2026 · Data refreshed monthly; last update November 19, 2026

The chart compared five economies. France had the highest youth unemployment of the five, at 18.9%. Among the 51 African countries with an estimate, 16 have a higher rate than France. South Africa's is 59.9%.

Why a low unemployment rate can hide a crisis

Several countries where Gen Z protests toppled or shook governments report youth unemployment below Germany's. That is not a sign of health. The unemployment rate counts only people with no work at all who are looking for it. Where there is no unemployment insurance, few young people can afford to be unemployed, so they take whatever informal work exists.

In Madagascar, where youth-led protests ended Andry Rajoelina's presidency in October 2025, youth unemployment is just 5.2%. But 84.4% of all workers are in vulnerable employment: working for themselves or unpaid in a family business, usually without a contract or social protection.

The youngest region on earth

In Sub-Saharan Africa, 40.4% of the population is under 15. In the European Union it is 14.2%. These are the young people who will enter the labor market over the next decade, and the main reason youth labor conditions on the continent matter well beyond it.

Methods and data

Data. All labor and population figures come from the World Bank's World Development Indicators, retrieved through its public API. Youth unemployment and vulnerable employment are International Labour Organization modeled estimates, which fill gaps between national labor force surveys. For countries with few recent surveys, such as Djibouti, Libya and Somalia, treat single-year values as approximate. Africa here means the World Bank's Sub-Saharan Africa region plus Algeria, Djibouti, Egypt, Libya, Morocco and Tunisia.

Updates. The data is checked monthly. When the World Bank revises its estimates, the changes are reviewed before they are published, and the figures in this text update with them. World Bank figures last updated November 11, 2026.

What is missing. The original chart's companion graphic used Eurostat data on young adults living with their parents. No comparable, annually updated series exists across African countries, so it is not reproduced here.
